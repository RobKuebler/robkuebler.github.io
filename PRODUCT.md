# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, senior engineering and data science leads, technical recruiters, conference organizers, and prospective freelance clients. They're browsing on desktop, in a professional mindset, spending 60-120 seconds forming a first impression. The question they're trying to answer: "Is this person worth a serious conversation?"

## Product Purpose

Robert's portfolio exists to make one thing undeniably clear: this is a senior practitioner, not a generalist. It should close the gap between Robert's actual caliber (published author, Kaggle expert, industry ML experience, teaching background) and the snap judgment a hiring manager makes in two minutes. Success means inbound interest from employers or clients in the 100k+ euro range, without Robert having to explain himself.

## Brand Personality

Precise, warm, and genuinely curious. The persona of a mathematician who chose to make complex things legible for others — more professor-with-a-sense-of-humor than consultant-in-a-suit.

Three words: **rigorous, human, generous.**

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS portfolio templates (identical card grids, gradient heroes, "Hi, I'm a developer" opener)
- Bootcamp-flavored portfolios (skills progress bars, "technologies I know" icon walls)
- Corporate consultant aesthetics (navy + gold, serif-heavy, stiff)
- Over-designed "creative developer" portfolios (canvas animations, WebGL backgrounds, cursor effects)
- Anything that buries the actual work behind visual noise

## Design Principles

1. **Scholarly warmth** — serif body text signals "I've thought deeply about this," not "I generated a slide deck."
2. **One powerful moment per idea** — no repeating the same stat in three places. Trust the reader.
3. **Content over decoration** — the writing and experience are the portfolio. Decoration should stay invisible.
4. **Approachability through clarity** — short sentences, real words, no jargon. Robert teaches; the site should too.
5. **Editing is design** — removing the third stat block, the identical card grid, the side-stripe border: restraint makes the remaining elements stronger.
6. **Signal seniority quietly** — the site should feel expensive because it's precise and confident, not because it shouts.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. No known specific user needs beyond standard desktop web accessibility.
